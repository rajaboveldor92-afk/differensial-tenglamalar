Characteristics and canonical form

[0:00] With the right choice of new variables a complicated equation takes a very simple form, called the canonical form. In this part we write the characteristic equation and reduce a hyperbolic equation to canonical form step by step, finding its general solution.

[0:19] The characteristic equation is a d y squared minus two b d x d y plus c d x squared equals zero. Note the minus sign in the middle. If a is not zero, d y over d x equals b plus or minus the square root of the discriminant, divided by a. The integral curves of these ODEs are called characteristics.

[0:43] In the hyperbolic case there are two real characteristic families and, in suitable new coordinates, the canonical form reduces to u xi eta equals F. The parabolic case has one real family, while the elliptic case has no real characteristics. An important qualification is that the PDE type is preserved under smooth real coordinate changes with nonzero Jacobian. Degenerate or singular transformations are not covered by that statement.
