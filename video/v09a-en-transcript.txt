Undetermined coefficients and resonance

[0:00] In this lesson we look at equations whose right side is not zero: for example a battery is connected to a circuit, or an external force acts on a spring. We split the general solution into two parts and find the particular solution from an almost ready template by looking at the right side.

[0:20] The basic structure: the general solution equals the general solution of the homogeneous equation plus one particular solution. The reason is linearity: the difference of two solutions satisfies the homogeneous equation. Initial conditions are always applied to the full sum, not to the homogeneous part alone.

[0:42] If the right side is a quasi-polynomial, built from an exponential, a polynomial, cosine and sine, we attach to it the control number sigma equal to alpha plus i beta. If sigma is not a characteristic root, the particular solution has the same shape as the right side. If sigma is a root of multiplicity s, we multiply the template by x to the power s. This is resonance.

[1:11] Example: y double prime plus y equals four sine x. This is a spring without friction whose forcing frequency equals its natural frequency. The characteristic roots are plus or minus i. The control number sigma is i, a simple root, so s equals one.

[1:31] Template: x times, in brackets, A cosine x plus B sine x. Note that although only a sine is on the right, the cosine is taken too. Differentiating twice and substituting, the left side becomes minus two A sine x plus two B cosine x.

[1:50] Match the coefficients: minus two A equals four and two B equals zero. So A is minus two, B is zero, and the particular solution is minus two x cosine x. The general solution is C one cosine x plus C two sine x minus two x cosine x.

[2:11] The graph shows the particular solution minus two x cosine x. Its amplitude grows linearly with x. This is what resonance looks like: the force pushes at just the right moment every time and the oscillation grows without bound. That is why real bridges and machines have dampers.
