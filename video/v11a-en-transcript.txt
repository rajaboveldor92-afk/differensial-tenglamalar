Euler’s method and its error

[0:00] Many differential equations cannot be solved by formulas. For example, y prime equals x squared plus y squared. In this lesson we learn to find the solution approximately, as a table of numbers. The simplest way is Euler’s method, and we will also measure how accurate it is.

[0:21] At the starting point the slope is known: it is the value of f there. We take a short step h along the tangent: the new y equals the old y plus h times f. Then we repeat from the new point. Joining the points gives the Euler polygon.

[0:39] Problem: y prime equals x plus y, y of zero equals one, step zero point one. First step: f at zero, one equals one, so y one equals one point one.

[0:53] Second step: the slope is zero point one plus one point one, that is one point two. So y two equals one point two two. Continuing for five steps, at x equal to zero point five we get about one point seven two one.

[1:11] The exact solution is y equals two e to the x minus x minus one; at zero point five it is one point seven nine seven. The error is about zero point zero seven six. Halving the step roughly halves the error: this is first order, big O of h.

[1:30] The graph shows the direction field and the exact integral curve through zero, one. The Euler polygon follows tangents of a convex curve and stays below it. The local error per step is proportional to h squared, but there are one over h steps, so the global error is proportional to h.
