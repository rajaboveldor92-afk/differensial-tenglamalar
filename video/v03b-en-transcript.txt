The mirror problem

[0:00] A headlight lamp sits at one point, yet the light leaves as a parallel beam. A satellite dish gathers a parallel signal into one point. In this part we find the shape of such a mirror with a differential equation. The result is beautiful: it is a parabola.

[0:19] The source is at the origin. The tangent to the mirror at a point M meets the x axis at T. By the law of reflection, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. Since the reflected ray is parallel to the x axis, triangle O M T is isosceles: the distance O T equals O M, the square root of x squared plus y squared.

[0:45] T lies to the left of O, so O T equals y over y prime minus x. Set this equal to the root and solve for y prime: y prime equals y over x plus the root of x squared plus y squared. Scaling x and y by the same number leaves the fraction unchanged, so the equation is homogeneous.

[1:06] Here it is easier to treat x as a function of y. After x equals v times y we get y times v prime equals the root of one plus v squared. Separate and integrate: the logarithm of v plus the root of one plus v squared equals the logarithm of y over C.

[1:25] Exponentiate and replace v by x over y: x plus the root equals y squared over C. Isolate the root, square, and cancel y squared. Answer: y squared equals two C x plus C squared. This is a parabola with focus at the origin, and the mirror is a paraboloid of revolution.

[1:48] The graph shows two parabolas, with C equal to one and two. All of them share one focus, the origin. The parameter C only changes the width of the mirror. When designing a dish engineers choose this parameter, and the receiver always sits at the focus.
